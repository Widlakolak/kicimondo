## Child-first visual design

- Preserve original cozy pixel art, 16x16 world tiles, all current location/quest/cat IDs, 25 distinct quest rewards and gates. Decouple world dimensions from the viewport using a dynamic camera. Prioritize readable sprites, discovery and comfortable gameplay over seeing the entire room at once. Compact HUD, readable contrast, generous hit areas, simple icons with short Polish labels and optional longer hints. Avoid decorative chrome and redundant prose. Use a simple sans-serif; target 18–22 CSS px for child instructions and 24+ for important numbers, adapt without overflow. Touch controls at least 48x48 CSS px, preferably 56–64 for primary actions. Keep keyboard navigation and meaningful accessible names.
- Owner clarification supersedes all earlier portrait-layout guidance: Kicimondo is a landscape-only game, not a responsive document-style app. Use a viewport-filling game shell without page chrome, scrolling, cards around the HUD or oversized margins. In normal landscape play, target a world-frame allocation of 60–70% of usable viewport width and nearly the full usable height, with a compact HUD/control dock in the remaining width. Treat this as a responsive allocation target, not a fixed canvas area percentage. The camera viewport fills the allocated game frame with a uniform world scale and adapts its visible world area to the actual aspect ratio. Center small maps and use a cohesive surround beyond map bounds; every walkable tile and interaction must remain reachable through camera movement. Do not stretch sprites or force a fixed map aspect ratio. Use safe-area-aware sizing and older WebView fallbacks; recompute on viewport changes without restarting the scene. The light/white frame contains only the room/world canvas. HUD (location, stars, remaining tasks, menu/fullscreen) is a separate layer or in the adjacent dock. Interactive overlay elements alone receive pointer input; avoid covering important targets, doors and player. Keep instructions short and UI game-like.
- Landscape is the only supported gameplay orientation. On native Android configure the game activity for sensor-landscape (both horizontal directions) and verify Back, lifecycle and accessibility; use landscape preference in the PWA manifest. Browser orientation lock is best-effort only when supported and permitted, requested from an explicit play/fullscreen user gesture; gracefully handle rejection/unavailability. On a portrait browser viewport show a simple large rotate-device icon and short Polish instruction ("Obróć ekran"), pause movement/simulation safely without resetting or discarding a save, and automatically resume when landscape returns. No portrait gameplay layout and no forced CSS rotation. Keep start/menu/install and system navigation accessible where needed. Do not trap a device that cannot rotate.
- Fullscreen: the game surface fills 100% of the usable fullscreen viewport; the dynamic camera viewport fills that surface, with uniform sprite scale. Wider viewports can expose more horizontal world space rather than stretching or simply shrinking sprites; a small map may still have neutral space beyond its boundaries. Do not force the entire map to fit on screen. No permanent side dock in fullscreen. Use independent floating HUD and optional compact control overlay/dock, with primary interaction button directly below D-pad. Explicit show/hide control toggle remains reachable, as do menu and exit fullscreen; hidden controls reserve no layout space. Tap-to-walk/tap-to-interact and keyboard work with controls hidden. Default to visible controls for first-time children, retain preference across room changes and fullscreen transitions in the session. Hide/show and orientation/fullscreen changes must clear held movement/routes when necessary and never recreate the scene, lose progress or activate a world tap. Use user-initiated fullscreen where supported, expanded viewport fallback otherwise; do not claim browser bars can always be hidden. Keep dialogs within fullscreen root and preserve Back/escape. Prefer landscape in all gameplay and challenge layouts; no automatic fullscreen.
- Show a persistent high-contrast star/outline above each incomplete quest's actual furniture/anchor whenever in view, even when the player is not nearby. Provide a compact, non-distracting directional cue toward the nearest unfinished reachable task outside the current view, with a room task count. Do not mark unreachable tasks as immediately actionable. Distinguish nearby target, unlocked transition and locked gate by shape/icon as well as color. Remove completed markers immediately after committed completion. No stars on unrelated decoration or completed quests. Add simple room task count and clear next-step hint when all tasks done. Avoid distracting flashing; reduced-motion support. Keep marker rendering lightweight on old WebView.
- Display points as stars with optional label "Gwiazdki samodzielności" and brief first-use explanation ("Zrób zadanie. Zdobądź gwiazdkę. Odkrywaj świat."). Internal PS semantics unchanged. Show +1 and short joyful feedback after committed completion only; exact newly unlocked locations based on previous/new total, not just next threshold. Gate hints show remaining stars and unlocked doors. Do not replay celebrations on reload or duplicate awards, and do not imply trust/discovery awards PS. Keep rewards idempotent and existing saves compatible.

## Dynamic camera and world layout

The owner explicitly approved a dynamic camera and prioritized readability and playability over a fixed map aspect ratio. This supersedes earlier whole-room/4:3 assumptions.

- Separate map/world coordinates from screen/camera coordinates in Phaser and React. Use approximately 384x216 logical pixels (16:9) as an initial full-screen camera reference, not a fixed map size or a mandatory zoom. Keep 16x16 world tiles and original sprite proportions. Derive camera zoom/visible bounds from the available game viewport; adapt visible horizontal/vertical extent to its aspect ratio, with bounded zoom chosen to keep the player, cats, quest objects and markers legible on a small landscape phone. In the narrower standard game frame, show a smaller portion of the world rather than shrinking everything to fit. Compare candidate zoom values during playtests and document the chosen limits. No pinch zoom is required.
- Follow the player with a bounded dead zone and gentle, predictable movement; do not make children fight camera drift. Snap to the correct camera position on initial spawn and room transition. Clamp within map bounds when the map exceeds the visible area and center smaller maps on each axis, avoiding void/overscroll and fake walkable areas. Respect reduced motion (no camera shake or unnecessary animated zoom). Avoid camera movement while a challenge/menu is open or the game is backgrounded; resume safely. Maintain pixel-art clarity with nearest-neighbor rendering and appropriate pixel rounding; avoid nonuniform scaling, excessive device-pixel-ratio cost and per-frame React state updates.
- Existing maps may remain 320x240 where playable. Allow focused enlargement/recomposition of existing rooms if it materially improves navigation or readability, but no new locations or quests and no automatic wholesale conversion of maps to 16:9. Preserve map identifiers, anchor/quest IDs, transition and spawn keys, content contract and save semantics. Validate collisions, furniture art, entrances, cat routes, quest reachability and all progression gates if any geometry changes. Existing saves must load at valid checkpoints without a reset.
- HUD and controls are screen-space elements independent of camera scroll/zoom. Markers attached to world objects must follow the world, not the HUD. Use a simple edge arrow/star for an off-screen reachable unfinished task; keep it distinct from the actual interaction target and do not activate quests remotely. Preserve the room task count and provide a clear direction to explore when targets are out of frame. Ensure the player and relevant nearby target stay visible and HUD controls do not intercept unrelated taps.
- Implement tap-to-walk/tap-to-interact using a single consistent screen-to-world transform (actual canvas bounds plus camera transform at the time of input); use world coordinates for hit testing, pathfinding and collision. Verify interaction range remains a world-space rule, never enlarged by zoom. Manual and automatic movement must work across camera scrolling. Resize/orientation/fullscreen/dock toggles update camera layout without remounting the world or losing progress.
- Keep performance suitable for the old MediaPad T5: bounded rendering resolution, no new heavy effects, no per-frame path search; measure representative large/small map cases and report actual browser/emulator results separately from physical device acceptance.
